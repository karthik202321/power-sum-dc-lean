CURRENT VERIFICATION SCOPE: ORIGINAL CONORMAL / GAUSS SPECIALIZATION
2026-10-10

A. FINAL EXACT AND BORDER STATEMENTS

The final exact and border endpoint modules compile.

The target is the literal Fermat polynomial sum_i X_i^n in n variables.
For n >= 3, an ordinary size-m affine-linear determinant representation,
or its border membership, implies

  (n-1)^2 / (4e) <= m.

For symmetric affine-linear determinant representations and their border,

  (n-1)^2 / (2e) <= m.

ConormalExactMultiplicity contains ordinary_exact_count,
symmetric_exact_count, ordinary_exact_quadratic and symmetric_exact_quadratic.
Their only mathematical inputs are n >= 3 and the corresponding literal
complex representation. Symmetric representations require actual symmetric
coefficient matrices. Positive matrix size is derived.

ConormalBorderMultiplicity contains ordinary_border_count,
symmetric_border_count, ordinary_border_quadratic and
symmetric_border_quadratic. Their only mathematical inputs are n >= 3 and
OrdinaryFiniteSupportBorder or SymmetricFiniteSupportBorder. This membership
means the target coefficient vector belongs to the Euclidean closure of the
actual determinant locus in some finite coefficient space. The AtSize
wrappers use the proved canonical support containing the target and every
size-m determinant. The Zariski count endpoints instead use the literal
Zariski-border predicates.

The count conclusions are fermatCount n <= ordinaryBezout m n and
fermatCount n <= symmetricBezout m n, with
fermatCount n = n*(n-1)^(n-2). The proved coefficient estimates give the
quadratic constants. None of these final statements accepts a family,
GaussDegreeTheory, genericity, finite-section, transversality, persistence,
Bezout, closure-comparison, cycle, or geometric count hypothesis.

The exact smooth homogeneous variants assume n >= 2, a nonzero homogeneous
polynomial of positive degree d, smoothness at its nonzero zeros, and a
literal representation. They prove d*(d-1)^(n-2) bounded by the appropriate
incidence coefficient. No claim for a larger class of polynomials is hidden
in the Fermat endpoint.

B. THE SPECIAL SECTION IS CONSTRUCTED, INCLUDING ITS FULL LENGTH

SmoothGaussPolars, GenericGaussPolars, IrrelevantSaturation and
GenericGaussPolarSequence prove gradient basepoint freeness and construct
successive polar flags by finite associated-prime avoidance. Irrelevant
saturation preserves sufficiently high graded pieces. The relevant
multiplication maps are proved regular; generic regularity is not an input.

SmoothGaussDegree computes the eventual Hilbert value from actual exact
sequences. SmoothGaussSection and SmoothGaussSectionLength construct a
support-avoiding linear form and an actual finite chart quotient.
ArtinianLocalDecomposition, FiniteQuotientPointFamily and SmoothGaussPointFamily
identify its dimension with the sum of the full local module lengths over
all its distinct support points. Nonreduced structure is retained.

ConormalGaussSection packages the flags, actual points, finite quotient,
Artinian local rings, smoothness, and length identity in a Section record.
The final exact and border proofs invoke its existence theorem, discharging
its hypotheses for Fermat. They do not ask the caller to supply a Section or
assume its degree. The special length sum is n*(n-1)^(n-2).

C. AN ACTUAL DVR FAMILY IS CONSTRUCTED FROM BORDER MEMBERSHIP

BorderDvrFamily proves the necessary continuous coefficient reindexing and
Euclidean-closure inclusion in Zariski closure. It does not assume that
Euclidean and Zariski closures agree for arbitrary sets, or use the reverse
inclusion.

AlgebraicCurveSelection and CurveThroughBoundary construct an algebraic
curve through the boundary point in the actual polynomial-image closure.
NormalGoingDown, CurveNormalization and FiniteCurveExtension supply a finite
normalized branch and local DVR, with rational residue field, uniformizer,
and the finite extension needed to carry a coherent generic preimage.
GenericPolynomialLift and AlgebraicBorderFamily construct that one tuple
of matrix parameters. The generic field is proved to be the fraction field
of the DVR. DvrRationalEntries clears all matrix-entry poles by one common
uniformizer power.

DeterminantDvrFamily reconstructs the coefficient polynomial and proves both
its literal generic determinant identity and its exact specialization to
Fermat. The border proof obtains either an exact representation or this
actual family. An arbitrary algebraic curve is never replaced by an assumed
parametrization over C[t].

D. FINITE GENERIC GAUSS SECTIONS WITH THE REQUIRED RESIDUES

ActualGenericGaussSection.exists_actual_section starts with the actual
family and the constructed special Section. It chooses dual and primal flag
coefficients in the DVR with precisely the special coefficients as residues.
RegularFlagsWithResidue proves that these choices can avoid the finitely many
associated primes: the perturbations are uniformizer multiples, and the
constant field is infinite. The primal normalization divides only by a
proved DVR unit and retains the prescribed residue.

The family polynomial is actually homogenized in one added coordinate.
DeterminantDvrHomogeneous proves its homogeneous and dehomogenization
identities, including the derivative identities. SaturatedSectionHilbert
and FinitePrescribedGaussSection use regular homogeneous cuts and Hilbert
polynomial degree descent to prove the eventual Hilbert function constant.
The added coordinate is regular on the saturated quotient, so its affine
chart is a finite-dimensional algebra.

HomogeneousDehomogenization and DehomogeneousGaussSection identify the
resulting actual affine ideal J. If Igen is the literal ideal of the generic
determinant, its n-2 chosen polar equations, and the chosen primal equation,
then the construction proves

  Igen <= J <= saturation(Igen, gradient(det A)),
  J = saturation(Igen, gradient(det A)).

It proves finiteness of L[x]/J over the actual generic field L and constructs
a unit linear combination of the derivatives in that full quotient.
SaturatedChartSmoothness supplies this unit from regularity and finiteness.
Consequently every geometric support point is smooth, also after extension
to an algebraic closure. The full unsaturated generic polar quotient is not
assumed finite and may have excess components.

This saturation is an ideal operation using power membership, not
radicalization. Localizing where one displayed derivative is a unit recovers
the original ideal exactly, preserving its nilpotents.

E. FULL SPECIAL LOCAL MULTIPLICITIES PERSIST

DVRRootPersistence starts from actual square equations and an isolated
Artinian special local ring. It derives a regular sequence and regularity
of the actual uniformizer on the total localized equation quotient. It then
cancels nonzero DVR denominators to prove that contraction from the generic
fibre preserves the special local equations.

DVRSaturatedPersistence extends the result to the generic saturated ideal.
At each chosen smooth special point some actual derivative has nonzero
value, so it is a unit in the full special local algebra. Explicit power
membership and unit cancellation show that every contracted generic equation
annihilates that whole algebra. DVRSaturatedGaussPersistence applies this to
the actual family and the residue-preserving flags, and also proves equality
of the actual localized ideals. It preserves local intersection length,
not merely a point or its residue field.

DVRSectionLength uses simultaneous interpolation onto the actual special
local algebras. The contraction of J gives a torsion-free DVR quotient.
DVRFibreDimension compares its special and finite generic dimensions;
it does not assume finite generation of the total module over the DVR or
infer generic finiteness from special finiteness. DVRGaussLengthBound
combines the constructions into

  fermatCount n <= finrank L (L[x]/J).

Generic finiteness is the independently constructed fact in part D.
No completion or local Zariski-main-theorem hypothesis is inserted.

F. INCIDENCE BEZOUT OVER THE ACTUAL GENERIC FIELD

KernelIncidenceAlgebra identifies normalized left/right kernel equations
with the determinant equation where an actual cofactor is nonzero.
KernelReducedIncidenceAlgebra and IncidenceReductionPoint prove the literal
reduced ideal equality using a constructed common reduction matrix.
The symmetric modules give the native one-vector kernel graph and two-factor
incidence chart. All these are actual quotient and localized-ring
identifications, preserving nilpotents and lengths.

The FieldPolarMultiplicityBound and FieldFinitePolarBound modules apply
these constructions over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field.
FieldSingularBezoutWithExcess bounds the sum of full local lengths for
selected projectively distinct isolated zeros of actual multihomogeneous
square equation systems. Its hypotheses specify valid charts, actual zeros,
Artinian local intersection rings, the displayed multidegrees, and the
ambient dimension count. These facts are proved for the actual incidence
systems. Excess components elsewhere are permitted. No simplicity, global
finiteness of the entire incidence scheme, or generic transversality is
assumed.

ArbitraryFieldFinitePolarBound extends the actual finite quotient L[x]/J
to an algebraic closure of L. Tensor/quotient isomorphisms preserve its
finite dimension. The unit derivative combination persists, and the
saturation sandwich identifies the original polar and J local rings at
all geometric support points. The proof uses the proved inclusion for
mapped saturations, not an assumed equality under arbitrary base change.
The actual function field is never identified with C.

The resulting upper bound is

  finrank L (L[x]/J) <= ordinaryBezout m n

or symmetricBezout m n. ConormalBorderMultiplicity combines it with part E
and the proved coefficient estimates. The exact proof applies the analogous
incidence bound directly to the constructed special section.

G. METHOD, LEGACY INTERFACES, AND WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED

The accurate method description is a local Gauss-section implementation of
conormal/Gauss specialization. It does not count a fixed Fermat polar slice
by setting an original variable to one. The generic chart x_0 = 1 sets the
ADDED HOMOGENIZING COORDINATE to one; the original polynomial variables
remain variables. The primal and dual flags are constructed as above.
This description identifies the mathematical route in the source. It does
not establish historical provenance or originality, or claim verbatim
formalization of every line of the manuscript's global cycle presentation.

The older ConormalExact GaussDegreeTheory and ConormalBorder.FamilyCertificate
interfaces remain conditional and are not claimed to be instantiated in
full generality. Their inputs include genuine geometric content. The final
ConormalExactMultiplicity and ConormalBorderMultiplicity endpoints use the
constructed local-section route instead. They do not require those legacy
certificates or UniversalPolynomialImageClosure.

Independent results on actual horizontal Gauss closure, flatness, conormal
containment, bihomogeneous ideals, bidegree conservation, projective ratio
charts, and torus-preserved generic local lengths remain in the development.
A separate global Chow ring, assembly of projective fibre cycles, and their
full intersection-theoretic interpretation are not claimed. They are not
undischarged hypotheses of the final numerical endpoints.

In particular, support containment alone is not used to assert the required
component multiplicity or dimension. Pointwise bigraded Hilbert inequalities
are not used to infer coefficientwise mixed leading-term inequalities.
The concrete local intersection rings are polynomial equation quotients
localized at actual evaluation points in valid projective charts; a separate
identification with stalks of a product of Mathlib Proj schemes is not claimed.
