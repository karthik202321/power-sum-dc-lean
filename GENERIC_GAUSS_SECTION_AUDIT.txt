ACTUAL GENERIC GAUSS SECTION: CONSTRUCTION AND HYPOTHESIS AUDIT

Principal endpoint:
  Sheshadri.ActualGenericGaussSection.exists_actual_section

Inputs are only the actual constructed determinant DVR family, 2 <= n,
and the already constructed special Fermat Gauss section. There is no
generic-flag, finite-section, transversality, regular-sequence, dimension,
count, smoothness, or local-ring-identification certificate among its inputs.

The endpoint constructs coefficients ell and a in the DVR, with their
residues equal pointwise to the special section's coefficients. It also
constructs an ideal J in the generic affine polynomial ring and coefficients
c in the actual generic representation field. It proves all of the following:

* The literal quotient by J is finite-dimensional over the generic field.
* I <= J <= saturation(I, gradient), where I is exactly the literal affine
  polar-chart ideal of the generic determinant, with the chosen dual flags
  and the normalized primal equation a.x - 1.
* The image of sum_i c_i partial_i(det A) is a unit in the full quotient by J.
* J is exactly saturation(I, gradient).

The unit statement retains nilpotents and forces every field-valued point
to be smooth. It also persists under coefficient-field extension. Thus the
later algebraic-closure incidence argument need not assume that arbitrary
base change commutes with saturation.

WHY THE GENERIC FLAGS EXIST

RegularFlagsWithResidue uses a moment-curve parameter in the embedded
constant field. For every associated prime, a nonzero coefficient
polynomial has finitely many bad parameters. Their finite union can be
avoided because the constant field is infinite. Each perturbation is a
multiple of the actual uniformizer, so every requested residue is retained.
This is an explicit construction from associated-prime avoidance, not an
assumption of genericity. The final primal coefficient at X0 is a proved
unit of the DVR with residue -1; multiplication by its negative inverse
normalizes that coefficient to exactly -1 in the DVR itself.

WHY THE GENERIC SECTION IS FINITE

The actual coefficient polynomial is homogenized. The initial principal
hypersurface ideal has Hilbert-polynomial degree at most n-1, with this
bound proved for its literal homogeneous quotient. Successive derivative
flags are regular on actual ideals saturated by X0 times the derivative
system. Each regular homogeneous cut lowers the Hilbert degree, and each
saturation only enlarges the ideal, so cannot increase the degree. After
n-2 derivative flags and one primal flag, the actual Hilbert function is
eventually constant. X0 is regular on the saturated quotient. The already
proved projective-chart theorem therefore gives a finite X0=1 chart.

HomogeneousDehomogenization supplies an actual quotient-algebra
isomorphism. DehomogeneousGaussSection identifies the mapped unsaturated
ideal with the literal affine polar equations. Its saturation comparison
maps explicit power-membership witnesses. The unit gradient combination
then upgrades the two ideal inclusions to exact saturation equality using
the radical of the actual annihilator of an element.

BOUNDARY AND SCOPE

Saturation removes components entirely supported where X0 or every
spatial derivative vanishes. At a point with X0 != 0 and a nonzero
derivative, the original and saturated localized ideals are equal as
ideals, preserving all local scheme multiplicities.

X0 is the added homogenizing coordinate. Setting X0=1 is ordinary
dehomogenization. No original Fermat variable is fixed to manufacture
an explicit Fermat polar point count. The special count remains the
constructed Gauss-section length n(n-1)^(n-2).

This module supplies the actual generic finite section required by the
border argument. The numerical border conclusion additionally uses the
separate DVR local-length comparison and arbitrary-field incidence bound.
