1. DEFORMATION AND PLAIN CONTRACTION

Let R=C[t], K=C(t), A=R[all homogeneous variables], and I=(F_e), where
F_e=(1-t)f_e+t*g_e and f_e,g_e have the SAME displayed multidegree. Set
J=comap(A -> K[variables])(I extended to K). This is the actual ideal used by
BezoutHorizontalFamily, without a radical.

The quotient A/J embeds in the actual generic quotient. Therefore it is
R-torsion-free even if the generic quotient is nonreduced, and flat over the
PID R. Homogeneous projection commutes with coefficient extension; homogeneous
generators, ideal extension and contraction give genuine multihomogeneity.
An actual graded piece is finite over R, and its inclusion in A/J has a
homogeneous projection as a retraction. Thus its base change still embeds even
for the nonflat map R -> C at a special parameter. FlatMultiGradedHilbert uses
the genuine scalar-compatible tensor-quotient equivalence, not just abstract
equal-dimensional vector spaces. This justifies dimension constancy in every
fixed tridegree.

Important inequality direction: I is contained in J, so at t=1 the split
equation ideal (g) is contained in J_1. Hence A_1/(g) SURJECTS onto A_1/J_1;
the degree-piece dimension of J_1 is at most that of (g), not the reverse.
No equality between the special fibre and the original split scheme is needed.
BezoutHorizontalFamily.originalIdeal_le_contraction establishes the required
unspecialized inclusion. Distinct evaluation maps at 0 and 1 must retain their
different R-algebra structures even though both target fields are C; the
current specialized_dimensions_equal theorem does this by passing through K.

2. WHY ONLY THE SELECTED SIMPLE ROOTS LIE IN J_0

Membership p in J gives a NONZERO denominator q(t) with q(t)*p in I. Simple
chart roots persist by the complex implicit-function theorem, keeping their
three selected nonzero chart coordinates fixed. Along the resulting branch,
q(t)*p(t,branch(t)) vanishes. The complement of the finitely many roots of q is
dense, so continuity proves p(0,root)=0 even when q(0)=0. BezoutRootPersistence
has this denominator argument, including the more general power version;
plain contraction can use exponent one.

One must not assert that every original special zero survives contraction.
For the elementary family t*x=0, the original t=0 equation is identically
zero, whereas generic contraction imposes x=0. The other special points do
not persist and are not simple. The selected-root/IFT premise is essential,
not a cosmetic strengthening of an automatic ideal inclusion.

The analytic theorem fixes ONE valid chart for EACH root. It does not require
all roots to use one common chart or every coordinate of every root to be
nonzero. Simplicity plus equation count makes the augmented derivative square
and invertible. Finite projective distinctness is proved through cross-products
if simultaneous branches are needed; the argument must not silently identify
affine distinctness with projective distinctness.

3. LOWER BOUND BY ACTUAL PROJECTIVE INTERPOLATION

ProjectivePointInterpolation.card_le_three_factor_piece_finrank applies to
the actual ideal J_0 once every selected point annihilates it. It uses genuine
nonzero blocks and pairwise projective nonequivalence. Products of linear
separators, padded to a common tridegree, give independent quotient classes.
Thus #P <= dim(A_0/J_0)_(k,k,k) for every k >= #P-1. Extra components and
nonreduced structure do not invalidate this lower bound.

Finiteness of the homogeneous piece is proved from finitely many monomials.
This matters: Lean's natural-valued finrank can be zero for an infinite-rank
module. The relevant proofs must use these actual finite-piece instances;
the entire multihomogeneous coordinate quotient is usually infinite-dimensional.

4. UPPER BOUND: PRODUCTS AND BRANCHES, WITHOUT REGULARITY

The safe elementary comparison is the following graded dimension inequality:
  H_(I+(a*b))(d+k) <= H_(I+(a))(d+k) + H_(I+(b))(k),
where a has degree d and all ideals/maps have the stated homogeneous meanings.
Multiplication by a maps the right shifted quotient to the middle quotient;
its image is the kernel of the projection to I+(a). The multiplication map
need not be injective. This gives an inequality, not a regular-sequence
equality. Iterating the inequality over finite products bounds the split
system by shifted pieces of all branch ideals.

ProjectiveChartHilbertBound.piece_finrank_mul_le now implements this comparison
and has directly compiled. Independent source review checks its explicit maps,
surjectivity of the projection, exact kernel=image identity, and finite-piece
dimension calculation. AddCancelCommMonoid supplies the needed degree
cancellation. Homogeneity of I is used exactly when extracting the component
of its error term. No regularity or reducedness hypothesis is concealed.

Each branch chooses one labeled factor from every equation. Use globally
distinct Vandermonde parameters within each projective block. If a branch
chooses more hyperplanes in a block than its projective dimension, enough
independent selected forms span ALL homogeneous coordinates of that block.
That branch's piece is zero whenever its degree in the block is positive.
This is a scheme-level ideal consequence, not merely a claim that it has no
projective points. If every block has exactly its dimension's quota, the
linear branch quotient has one surviving variable per block, so each fixed
tridegree piece has dimension at most one. The equation-count equality ensures
that every nonquota branch overloads at least one block.

Every iteration shifts a degree by a finite sum of factor degrees. Choose the
final diagonal k larger than ALL componentwise shifts and all branch thresholds,
as well as #P-1. A statement about sufficiently large TOTAL degree would not
suffice. The branch set is finite, so one uniform componentwise bound exists.

BezoutSplitBudget.coefficientBudget_eq_card expands the actual class product
and identifies the budget with the number of quota choices. Empty factor rows
(degree zero), zero-dimensional projective factors, and an empty chosen-point
set must remain included; the counting API already treats empty rows correctly
as product 1, with no possible branch choice.

5. TWO TEMPTING BUT INVALID SHORTCUTS

Full Cox-ring regularity is false even for a square split start system. The
new kernel-checked BezoutExcessRegressions module gives an explicit example
on three projective lines:
  g1=x0*y0*z0, g2=x1*y1*z1,
  g3=(x0+x1)*(y0+y1)*(z0+z1).
All have actual weight (1,1,1). Modulo (g1,g2), h=y0*z0*y1*z1 is nonzero but
g3*h=0. The theorem thirdEquation_not_regular_mod_first_two proves the
nonregularity. The irrelevant locus x0=x1=0 explains the obstruction. This
rules out substituting a full-ring regular-sequence Hilbert formula without
checking its hypotheses. It does not contradict chart regularity or Bezout.

The earlier CRT/chart alternative also requires care. BezoutExcessProducts
proves an exact product-ideal/intersection equality and actual CRT quotient
under explicit pairwise comaximality of branch ideals. This is appropriate
AFTER affine normalization, where quota branch quotients are copies of C and
impossible branches are the unit ideal. Homogeneous branch ideals are generally
not comaximal: they share the cone origin. Likewise, no projective points
outside a chosen chart does not alone make the original graded quotient map
injective in every degree; irrelevant torsion may remain. The latest product-
inequality route avoids needing that chart-injectivity theorem altogether.

The compiled concrete chain is
  #P <= H(J_0,k) = H(J_1,k) <= H((g),k) <= #quotaChoices = budget.
Every H denotes the actual trigraded quotient-piece construction, with scalar
structures and degree shifts explicitly transported. None of the
steps requires the original or total projective zero locus to be finite.
