A. ACTUAL CHART ALGEBRAS AND GENERIC MULTIPLICITIES

ConormalProjectiveCharts starts with a genuinely bihomogeneous ideal I in
R[x_i,y_j]. It proves that the literal normalized chart algebra

  R[x_i,y_j]/(I,x_i-1,y_j-1)

is algebra-isomorphic to the actual coordinate-ratio subalgebra in
(R[x_i,y_j]/I)[1/(x_i*y_j)]. The kernel equality is an equality of ideals;
no radicalization or supplied chart certificate replaces the equations.
ConormalChartTorus constructs both maps and proves the inverse laws for

  (R[x_i,y_j]/I)[1/(x_i*y_j)]
    ≃ (R[x_i,y_j]/(I,x_i-1,y_j-1))[u,v,1/(u*v)].

ActualProjectiveFibreCharts applies this to the actual specialized ideal
ActualGaussFamily.fibreIdeal, using its proved bihomogeneity. The fibre ideal
retains its nilpotents. The module constructs Spec of each actual chart ring
and its affine fundamental cycle, with weights given by actual minimal-prime
local-ring lengths, positive weights, and whole-spectrum support.

B. FINITE PROJECTIVE SECTIONS: HILBERT VALUES EQUAL ALL LOCAL LENGTHS

FiniteProjectiveHilbert treats a homogeneous ideal in three coordinate blocks
and actual linear forms lx, lu, lv of the corresponding multidegrees. Its
BoundaryAvoidance hypothesis is the literal ideal condition

  every Segre coordinate Xi*Uj*Vk belongs to radical(I + (lx*lu*lv)).

This is a support condition, not a count or degree certificate. From it the
module proves eventual bijectivity between diagonal homogeneous quotient
pieces and the actual quotient by lx-1, lu-1, lv-1. Finite-dimensionality of
that quotient is a conclusion. No reducedness is required.

ArtinianLocalDecomposition proves that a commutative Artinian ring is the
product of its actual localizations at ALL maximal ideals. For a finite
algebra over an algebraically closed field, it derives the residue-field
identifications and equality between dimension and the sum of actual local
module lengths. No selected list of roots or assumed CRT isomorphism enters.

ProjectiveHilbertLocalLengths combines these results. Its
  eventually_hilbert_eq_sum_local_lengths
identifies every sufficiently high diagonal piece dimension with the full
sum over maximal ideals of the normalized chart. Its
  chartCycleDegree_eq_of_boundary
also proves equality of this numerical degree for any two common charts
satisfying the stated support condition. This numerical chart independence
does not construct a globally glued projective cycle.

OrdinaryProjectiveHilbert proves the corresponding ordinary graded result:
an eventually constant actual Hilbert function and a filter-regular linear
form yield eventual piece isomorphisms with K[Z]/(I,l-1), finiteness of that
quotient, and equality of the constant value with its full sum of local
lengths. Filter-regularity means eventual injectivity of actual multiplication

SmoothGaussSectionLength applies the constructed derivative-polar section to

AffineIncidenceLocalCharts proves that the literal dehomogenization quotient

C. TRUE TOP MIXED-COEFFICIENT COMPARISON

MonomialOrthantDecomposition constructs an explicit finite disjoint partition
of all exponent vectors. Truncating each coordinate at a common bound for
finite monomial divisibility tests gives finitely many cells; each cell is
literally a translate of a coordinate orthant. The cellFiberEquiv and
exponentEquivCells equivalences retain every exponent exactly once. The
orthantPolynomial_eval theorem identifies the resulting polynomial with the
actual bidegree count. Its summands are products of shifted stars-and-bars
polynomials, with positive coefficients at their own top bidegrees.

The important distinction is between top coefficients and lower terms:
lower coefficients of a shifted stars-and-bars polynomial may be negative.
active_cell_degree_le proves that a surviving orthant cannot hide above a
supplied total-degree bound through cancellation. From this,
orthantPolynomial_top_coeff_nonneg proves positivity on the top total-degree
face, rather than falsely deriving it from nonnegative evaluations.

MixedHilbertComparison.top_mixed_coefficient_mono is the resulting theorem
for actual equation ideals. For bihomogeneous I <= J in K[x_i,y_j], actual
eventual Hilbert polynomials HI and HJ, and total degree of HI at most d,

  coefficient_(i,j)(HJ) <= coefficient_(i,j)(HI) whenever d <= i+j.

The standard-monomial equivalences for the actual quotients and one common
monomial order supply a shared nested cell decomposition. The theorem has
no cycle, effectivity, coefficient-positivity, or desired-count hypothesis.
The stated total-degree bound is still a genuine hypothesis of this general
theorem; it is not automatically an established fact for every Gauss family.
Its coefficients are literal polynomial coefficients, not independently
constructed global intersection numbers.

  to saturation because saturation enlarges the original ideal.
- regular_cut_natDegree_le and filterRegular_cut_natDegree_le: an actual

- filterRegular_cut_eq_zero_of_natDegree_eq_zero: a further filter-regular

  S0 = saturation(I, opens),
  S(j+1) = saturation(Sj + (gj), opens).

E. SCOPE OF THE LOCAL SPECIALIZATION ROUTE

The numerical construction can compare special Gauss-section local lengths
with a generic finite section and apply the isolated-zero incidence bound.
These are literal local equation algebras; singular section points keep
their full lengths. This route supplies the algebraic conormal-specialization
comparison using finite sections, rather than assuming a projective cycle
intersection formula for the constructed family.

The following global formalizations are not claimed by these modules:

- Gluing all projective charts and their fundamental cycles into a global
  biprojective conormal family, with an identification with a chosen Proj
  or scheme-theoretic conormal construction.
- A general purity/minimal-component theorem for the special Gauss fibre.
  Support containment by itself does not prove component membership.
- Identifying every top mixed Hilbert coefficient with a separately defined
  global mixed intersection number, together with global cycle additivity.

These global statements must not be reported as proved merely because the
finite local-section route yields a numerical conclusion. Conversely, their
absence does not erase the actual ideal, local-ring, Hilbert-comparison, and
finite-section theorems listed above. Final theorem statements and the
integrated audit determine which numerical border conclusions are closed.

Curve normalization, the actual DVR family, Gauss containment, and graded
fibre-dimension conservation have their own proved modules. They are not
assumed analytic closure certificates. Extension-field incidence bounds and
the final local specialization assembly are separate modules; this document
does not substitute for their actual theorem statements.

The chart normalizations and varying derivative flags described here stay
within the conormal-specialization route. They do not use a fixed Fermat
coordinate slice to count solutions. This is a structural statement about
the development, not a historical originality claim about classical methods.

No assertion is made that arbitrary nonregular equations satisfy a
complete-intersection Bezout equality. The excess-compatible Bezout bound
uses actual isolated local lengths and permits other components.
