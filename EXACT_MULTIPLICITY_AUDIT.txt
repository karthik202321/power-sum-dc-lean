SEMANTIC AUDIT: CONSTRUCTED EXACT CONORMAL MULTIPLICITY PIPELINE
Date: 2026-10-10

Scope and verdict

I reviewed the actual definitions, theorem statements, and applications in
SmoothGaussPointFamily, ConormalGaussSection, OrdinaryIncidenceLocalMultiplicity,
SymmetricIncidenceLocalMultiplicity, and ConormalExactMultiplicity. I also
followed their section-length, kernel-lift, chart, and singular Bezout inputs.
This is a semantic source audit, separate from the final whole-project build
and axiom audit. The final endpoint must compile before its verification is
claimed.

No remaining paper-specific geometric hypothesis is present in the NEW exact
endpoints as written. In particular, ordinary_smooth_degree_bound and
symmetric_smooth_degree_bound construct the section, its length, its incidence
lifts, and the required local-ring identifications. Their inputs are a literal
affine determinant representation, positive-degree nonzero homogeneity, and
the ordinary smoothness condition away from the origin; the symmetric theorem
also assumes literal transpose symmetry of every coefficient matrix.

The Fermat endpoints ordinary_exact_count, symmetric_exact_count,
ordinary_exact_quadratic, and symmetric_exact_quadratic take only n >= 3 and
the corresponding literal representation predicate. They do not accept
GaussDegreeTheory, a count budget, a numerical multiplicity certificate,
transversality, a supplied section, or a supplied local-ring isomorphism.

This verdict concerns the exact theorem. It does not establish the border
theorem, curve selection, or every cycle/specialization statement in the paper.

1. What is actually represented

PolynomialModel.affinePencil is A_0 + sum_i A_(i+1) X_i, and determinant is its
literal polynomial determinant. ExactRepresentation asserts the existence of
such matrices with determinant equal to sum_i X_i^n as a polynomial, rather
than equality of zero sets. SymmetricExactRepresentation additionally asserts
A_i.transpose = A_i. This is complex symmetric, not Hermitian, symmetry.

The smooth-form endpoint assumes f is homogeneous of degree d, f != 0, d > 0,
and every nonzero zero of f has some nonzero formal partial derivative. That
is the standard smoothness condition for the projective hypersurface in
characteristic zero. No claim is made for arbitrary singular hypersurfaces.
Singular isolated SECTION intersections are allowed throughout.

2. The Gauss section and its number are constructed

The actual section ideal is

  (f, ell_1 . grad(f), ..., ell_(n-2) . grad(f), a . x - 1).

SmoothGaussPolars derives the needed basepoint-free gradient ideal statement
from Euler's identity, smoothness, and Nullstellensatz. GenericGaussPolars and
GenericGaussPolarSequence select actual coefficient vectors by associated-
prime avoidance on the irrelevant saturation. They produce filter-regular
sections of the literal original ideals. SmoothGaussDegree then computes the
eventual Hilbert dimension as d(d-1)^(n-2); it does not assume this number.

SmoothGaussSection constructs a linear form a avoiding the entire projective
support of that finite polar section. SmoothGaussSectionLength proves that
the literal affine quotient with a . x = 1 is finite and has that dimension.
SmoothGaussPointFamily obtains a complete finite list from its maximal
spectrum and proves the sum of the actual local-ring lengths equals that
dimension. The list's completeness, finiteness, and multiplicity sum are
conclusions, not hidden assumptions on a chosen list of roots.

Section is a data structure containing a length_sum field, so the helper
ordinary_section_bound is deliberately conditional on such data and on local
isomorphisms. That would not alone close the theorem. The final smooth-form
endpoints explicitly call exists_section, construct the lift families, and
construct the local isomorphisms. The final exact endpoints therefore do not
merely repackage the desired geometric count as an argument.

3. Incidence and local multiplicities are literal

For each selected base point, GenericKernelLifts derives a nonzero adjugate
entry from its nonzero determinant derivative, then constructs normalized
left and right kernel vectors. The ordinary construction chooses ONE common
reduction matrix Lambda for the finite family, with proved injectivity on
each hyperplane u . z = 0. A different Lambda is not silently used at every
point in the global Bezout system.

The orientation is consistent. If i is rightIndex and j is leftIndex, then
alpha = adj(A)_(i,j), v_i = 1, u_j = 1, and adj(A) = alpha v u. Thus

  trace(adj(A) H) = alpha (u H v).

The local graph argument uses precisely that unit alpha. Note that an
adjugate entry is the transposed classical cofactor; the code consistently
uses adjugate entries, so no extra transposition is needed.

KernelIncidenceAlgebra constructs an actual quotient-ring isomorphism between
the normalized full kernel incidence and R/(det A), over arbitrary
commutative rings. Its cut versions identify the bilinear polar cuts with
the trace-adjugate cuts as ideals. They retain nilpotents. The nonzero
cofactor is turned into a unit by localization at the actual base point.

For the ordinary reduced system, the matrix with first row u and remaining
rows Lambda is invertible at every selected point. This follows from the
constructed reduction injectivity. KernelReducedIncidenceAlgebra proves
equality of the full and reduced localized equation ideals. It does not
merely prove equality of their reduced zero sets or tangent spaces.

AffineIncidenceLocalCharts transports the actual evaluation prime through
dehomogenization. NestedPolynomialLocalPoint and
KernelIncidenceLocalMultiplicity then identify the actual full-point local
ring, not just a coefficient-localized surrogate, with the actual base
polar LocalIntersectionRing. The symmetric route constructs the analogous
one-vector graph using a proved nonzero diagonal adjugate entry.

ConormalPolarIdeal proves equality of the base trace-polar ideal with the
original derivative-polar section ideal using Jacobi's identity. Accordingly,
isolatedness and the FULL singular multiplicity transfer by ring isomorphism.
No simplicity, reducedness, Jacobian rank, or tangent-kernel premise remains.

4. Bezout is not stated too strongly

SingularBezoutWithExcess.isolated_multiplicity_bound takes a square collection
of actual multihomogeneous equations, valid nonzero projective chart
coordinates, a finite list of actual roots, Artinian local rings at those
selected roots, and projective distinctness. It bounds the sum of their
actual module lengths by the multihomogeneous coefficient budget.

It does NOT require the global common zero scheme to be finite, reduced,
equidimensional, or a proper intersection everywhere. It does NOT assume the
selected list contains every global incidence root. Positive-dimensional
excess elsewhere is permitted. Thus there is no illicit use of the stronger
and generally false assertion that an arbitrary excess intersection has
total scheme length equal to the Bezout coefficient.

The theorem's comparison uses a constructed interpolation/contraction and a
proved local persistence result at the isolated roots. The final statement
does not carry flatness, conservation, or a closure hypothesis as an input.
The exact endpoint itself has no closure hypothesis to audit. Separate
border-family closure statements are outside this audit.

5. Dimension, degree, and normalization checks

Write m for matrix size and n for the number of original variables.

Ordinary ambient space: P^n x P^(m-1) x P^(m-1), dimension n+2m-2.
Equations: m left-kernel, m-1 reduced right-kernel, n-2 dual flags, one primal
flag, totaling n+2m-2. Their multidegrees are respectively (1,1,0), (1,0,1),
(0,1,1), and (1,0,0). The proved class product is

  X (X+U)^m (X+V)^(m-1) (U+V)^(n-2).

Its coefficient of X^n U^(m-1) V^(m-1) is exactly ordinaryBezout m n.

Symmetric ambient space: P^n x P^(m-1), dimension n+m-1.
Equations: m kernel, n-2 dual, one primal, totaling n+m-1; their bidegrees
are (1,1), (0,2), and (1,0). The coefficient is
2^(n-2) choose(m,n-1). The three-factor Bezout backend adds only a P^0 factor.
BiProjectiveLocalMultiplicity proves that removing its coordinate via z=1
preserves the actual local ring, so no multiplicity factor is lost or gained.

Each projective chart fixes one nonzero coordinate per block to its actual
value. In the constructed charts these values are all 1. The three ordinary
or two symmetric chart equations remove exactly the scaling dimensions; they
are not extra geometric cuts of the projective intersection.

Projective distinctness follows from injectivity of the base point list and
the common first coordinate 1. The argument does not count the same
projective point repeatedly through different kernel normalizations.

6. Edge cases and constants

The general smooth-form results require n >= 2 and d > 0. The case n=2 uses
no polar cuts and has section length d. For d=1 and n>2 the target is zero;
an empty polar section is allowed. For n=2,d=1 the empty-product convention
gives 0^0=1, as required. No unintended n-2 truncation is used below n=2.

Matrix size zero is ruled out from the actual root at the origin: the empty
matrix determinant is 1, whereas a positive-degree homogeneous f vanishes
there. For m=1 the reduction has zero rows, and the normalized left row alone
gives the required one-by-one invertible augmented matrix. The formal
successor-size chart lemmas cover this case as their m=0 index parameter.

The Fermat corollaries require n>=3 and degree d=n. Their proved section
length is n(n-1)^(n-2). The existing numerical estimates then yield exactly

  ordinary:  (n-1)^2 / (4 exp(1)) <= m,
  symmetric: (n-1)^2 / (2 exp(1)) <= m.

The constants are consequences of the newly constructed exact count bounds;
they are not hypotheses fed into those bounds.

7. Original route, coordinate charts, and provenance

This is a Gauss-polar/conormal-incidence proof of the exact theorem. Its
generic section is built for EVERY smooth homogeneous form before Fermat is
specialized. The additional projective cone coordinate is fixed to 1 to
describe an affine chart. The primal equation a . x = 1 is chosen after the
polar section so that it covers the section's entire projective support.

No endpoint calls a fixed-coordinate Fermat slice theorem, enumerates a
special root-of-unity slice, or sets one distinguished ORIGINAL Fermat
variable x_(n-1) to 1 to obtain the lower count. A projective coordinate
normalization and a constructed generic linear form should not be confused
with that special explicit-slice argument.

This code inspection establishes which route the proof uses; it cannot
establish historical priority or rule out overlapping standard ideas in
other authors' work. It would be too strong to infer historical independence
merely from a clean import or axiom audit.

8. Remaining scope limits and reporting recommendation

Once the new exact endpoints compile and pass their final axiom audit, the
exact ordinary and symmetric statements have no remaining geometric
certificate or textbook-theorem hypothesis. The old conditional theorems in
ConormalExact and its GaussDegreeTheory interface may remain in the repository,
but they are not the source of the new exact bounds. The new module imports
ConormalExact for proved elementary Fermat properties, not an inhabitant of
that interface. Refer to ConormalExactMultiplicity by name when reporting
the unconditional result.

Do not expand this into a claim that every sentence of the paper's original
proof has been formalized verbatim. The exact route now uses explicit
scheme-level Gauss sections, Hilbert dimensions, and local-ring equivalences
in place of the older abstract conormal intersection-degree interface. It
preserves the conormal-incidence argument and handles singular section
multiplicities. Border/specialization endpoints, their actual family
construction, and their remaining hypotheses require a separate review.
