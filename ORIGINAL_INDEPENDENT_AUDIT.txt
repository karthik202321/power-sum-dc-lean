INDEPENDENT SEMANTIC AUDIT OF THE ORIGINAL CONORMAL ROUTE

Scope: OriginalConormal.lean and its local transitive imports, including
ConormalCone, ConormalPersistence, ConormalZariski, ConormalTopology,
ConormalFlatness, MultiplicityOne, ConormalHilbert, ConormalEulerHilbert,
ConormalCycles, ConormalSpecialization, ConormalIncidence, ConormalExact,
and ConormalBorder. This is a source-level semantic review. Compilation
and Lean axiom-dependency reports are separate evidence.

OVERALL CONCLUSION

The new entry point is independent of the previous explicit Fermat polar
slice. It verifies substantial algebraic and analytic ingredients and a
conditional assembly following the conormal-specialization argument.
It does NOT verify the complete original proof from bare exact or border
representation hypotheses using only the previously stated Bezout input.

The original-route exact results additionally require an unconstructed
GaussDegreeTheory. The original-route border results additionally require
an unconstructed FamilyCertificate, positive matrix size, and fibrewise
determinant representations over its good parameters. No theorem here
constructs that certificate from Euclidean or Zariski border membership.
This limitation is visible in the final theorem signatures and is stated
in the source documentation.

No false mathematical theorem or inconsistent universal hypothesis was
found in this review. No custom axiom declaration, sorry, admit, or unsafe
declaration was found in the reviewed new source. That does not discharge
its proposition-valued arguments and certificate fields.

IMPORT INDEPENDENCE

The source import graph of OriginalConormal.lean contains 42 local modules
at the time of review. It has no transitive import of any Explicit* module,
ExpandedExact, ExpandedBorder, BorderPolarFamily, BorderIncidence, or the
Fermat-specific ExactKernelLifts module. GenericKernelLifts is used instead.
The shared PolynomialFamilies/StableFiniteFibres material supplies a
general inverse-function theorem argument, not a preselected Fermat slice.

ZariskiBridge is imported for its polynomial closure definitions and easy
Euclidean-to-Zariski inclusion. Importing its definition of
UniversalPolynomialImageClosure does not supply that proposition as a
proof. The original-route final statements do not require that principle;
they also do not construct a family from border membership.

WHAT IS PROVED ABOUT ACTUAL POLYNOMIALS AND MATRICES

1. The determinant-side incidence work is substantive. Literal affine
   matrix pencils, their evaluated derivatives, nonzero cofactors, actual
   normalized kernel vectors, and adjugate factorizations are used. The
   full homogeneous Gauss covector, including its zero-th coordinate, is
   identified with the gradient of the literal homogeneous determinant.
   General dual flags have the chart equation
       sum_i (ell_i - ell_0*x_i) * partial_i f = 0.
   The coefficient ell_0 is unrestricted. The first-order elimination and
   normalized kernel uniqueness prove incidence chart simplicity. Given
   actual finite transverse roots, the universal Bezout input bounds their
   number in both ordinary and symmetric models.

2. ConormalPersistence proves local persistence at every actual smooth
   polynomial zero: a coordinate with nonzero partial derivative supplies
   an invertible square system, and the inverse function theorem gives a
   continuous local branch with nonzero differential. The entire Gauss
   covector converges. Euler's identity identifies that covector with the
   differential of any homogeneous polynomial having the given affine
   restriction, at roots in the chart x0=1.

3. ConormalZariski proves containment of the closure of the target's
   scaled smooth Gauss representatives in the special fibre of any supplied
   closed total family satisfying its graph-containment and algebraicity
   hypotheses. The target containment is a conclusion, not a field assumed
   verbatim. The total family itself is not constructed by this theorem.

4. ConormalFlatness proves actual local ring/module algebra: a reduced
   ring injects into the product of its minimal-prime quotients;
   componentwise injectivity of the base implies torsion-freeness; and an
   arbitrary torsion-free module over a PID is flat, without a finite-module
   restriction. This is useful for the DVR argument. Applying these lemmas
   to the local rings of a constructed horizontal Gauss family, and then
   obtaining a flat scheme morphism, is not done here.

5. MultiplicityOne proves that a nonzero differential excludes a squared
   vanishing factor and, for a supplied finite factorization, selects a
   unique vanishing factor with exponent one. It does not construct the
   irreducible-component decomposition or fundamental fibre cycle.

6. ConormalCone proves the power-sum gradient and Euler identities,
   smoothness away from the vertex, and a scaling-compatible representative
   equivalence between vectors and a hyperplane avoiding the vertex. This
   is not yet an identification of conormal schemes or a proof of the
   cone multidegree shift or classical dual-degree formula.

GAUSS DEGREE THEORY: VALID BUT UNCONSTRUCTED GEOMETRY

GaussDegreeTheory assigns a natural number degree f to every polynomial.
It requests a GenericGaussSection containing that many distinct smooth
transverse roots, using one primal and n-2 unrestricted dual flags. The
section data are actual polynomial equations and actual derivatives.
They do not assert that the listed roots exhaust an independently defined
generic intersection. The upper-bound proof only requires their existence,
so this weaker section interface is logically sufficient.

The smooth_homogeneous_degree field says that for n>=3, d>=2, and a
nonzero homogeneous polynomial in n variables whose projective hypersurface
is smooth, the degree is d*(d-1)^(n-2). This has the correct exponent and
normalization for the (n-2)-nd conormal multidegree of the projective cone:
one primal hyperplane removes the cone direction and n-2 dual hyperplanes
give the smooth hypersurface's classical dual degree. No counterexample
was found under its characteristic-zero, dimension, and smoothness premises.

The intended invariant must use the literal polynomial's nonzero-gradient
locus, hence multiplicity-one components. For a pure repeated polynomial
such as g^2, that locus is empty and the section law forces degree zero.
The interface cannot mean the full conormal of the reduced underlying
hypersurface for every polynomial. This agrees with the paper's use of
the multiplicity-one Gauss graph rather than the full reduced conormal.

Nevertheless, degree is an abstract function constrained only by the two
fields. No inhabitant of GaussDegreeTheory, no intersection theory, and no
identification of this function with the paper's actual multidegree is
constructed. Fermat's homogeneity, nonzero polynomial, and smoothness
hypotheses are proved; its numerical degree then uses the supplied general
degree law. Calling the resulting exact theorem unconditional would be
incorrect.

AFFINE REPRESENTATIVES VERSUS PROJECTIVE CONORMAL GEOMETRY

ConormalZariski.conormalClosure is literally the affine polynomial-Zariski
closure of pairs
       (mu*(1,z), nu*(-sum z_i*partial_i f(z), gradient f(z)))
at smooth roots, with mu and nu nonzero. It includes boundary points with
zero coordinate blocks. No projective quotient, projective closed subscheme,
or fundamental conormal cycle is constructed by this definition.

The intended identification requires restricting to the two nonzero
blocks, quotienting the two scalar actions, and proving the appropriate
chart-density/closure statement. For the irreducible Fermat cone the affine
chart is the expected dense chart, but that geometric theorem remains a
bridge rather than a consequence already formalized here.

For a general size-m homogeneous determinant, the affine chart can omit a
multiplicity-one component x0=0 created by degree padding. The paper's
Remark 6.1 explains that this component has zero multidegree in slots k>=1.
Any identification with the full projective Gauss graph must establish
that fact or explicitly use the chart closure invariant. The generic and
target degree-identification fields currently carry such identifications.

A nonempty affine bi-cone has dimension two more than its projectivization.
The FamilyCycleData documentation now correctly distinguishes its affine
support dimension from its projective degree/Hilbert dimension d=n-1.
No equation erroneously identifies the two dimensions in the proofs.
However, scalar invariance of component sets, their actual dimensions, and
their interpretation as cones of projective fibre components are not
enforced or constructed by the record.

TOPOLOGY CORRECTION

The standard topology of complex coordinate space is Euclidean. Its
nontrivial algebraic sets are not irreducible in the topological sense
used by the generic component lemma, since that topology is Hausdorff.
The earlier risk is resolved in the integrated statements:
ConormalTopology constructs a separate explicit algebraicTopology, proves
that its closed sets are exactly polynomial-closure fixed points, and
identifies its closure operation with zariskiClosure.

Persistence uses the unchanged Euclidean topology. Target irreducibility
and component closedness explicitly use algebraicTopology. The topology
is not installed globally, so the two arguments are not conflated.
Algebraic irreducibility of the actual Fermat conormal remains a supplied
certificate field; it is not proved merely by defining this topology.

HILBERT AND CYCLE BOUNDARY

ConormalCycles defines effective cycles as finitely supported natural
weights on labels. It proves additivity, monotonicity, and the component
contribution inequality. Its finite-closed-cover irreducibility argument
is also proved. Equal dimension and an explicit strict dimension-drop
hypothesis then give equality with a supported component. These are
correct general arguments; component labels and weights are not thereby
identified with scheme components or generic local-ring lengths.

ConormalHilbert.topHilbertPolynomial is an injective factorial-normalized
encoding of a supplied multidegree vector. Equality of that encoding is
exactly equivalent to equality of the vector. Alone, this does not prove
geometric conservation from flatness.

ConormalEulerHilbert makes the boundary more explicit: integer-valued Euler
data and full polynomials are independently supplied, rather than defined
from multidegrees. It proves that equality on the natural-number grid
determines the full polynomial, and extracts the q^d coefficient after the
substitution (a,b)=(q,q*t). The coefficient has the correct factorial
normalization and retains all multidegrees.

The external data still include the evaluation identity with the supplied
integer values, the identification of the top coefficient with intersection
numbers, and the hypothesis hChi that those integer values are constant.
No sheaf cohomology or geometric Euler characteristic is constructed, and
proper flatness is not proved to imply hChi. Thus the numerical implication
from constant Euler data is verified, while the geometric conservation
theorem remains an obligation.

FAMILY CERTIFICATE AND FINAL STATEMENTS

FamilyCertificate supplies a polynomial family, a continuous parameter map,
a base point specializing to Fermat, good parameters accumulating at that
base, and a Euclidean closed total set containing their smooth Gauss graphs.
It further supplies a special-fibre support description, Zariski
irreducibility/closedness, dimension laws, independent Euler/Hilbert data
with constancy, and target/generic degree identifications.

It does not itself require a scheme, a smooth algebraic curve, a regular
parameter map, a proper or flat morphism, genuine irreducible fibre
components, scalar-invariant component sets, or actual local-length
multiplicities. Its fields record numerical/set-theoretic consequences
that the intended geometric construction must justify. In particular,
dimension_drop is a supplied no-proper-containment law for the chosen
dimension function, not a derived theorem of algebraic dimension theory.

ConormalSpecialization derives target containment and then the degree
comparison from these individual premises. ConormalBorder combines it
with the general section law, literal determinant incidence, universal
Bezout, and proved arithmetic. This is a valid conditional proof; the
certificate does not contain the final count inequality verbatim. It
still carries substantial paper-specific construction obligations.

ConormalFlatness and MultiplicityOne are available supporting lemmas, but
the final FamilyCertificate proof does not instantiate them on a geometric
family: its Euler constancy and degree-identification fields are supplied
directly. Their presence among imports must not be described as completing
the missing scheme-to-cycle application.

RECOMMENDED DISCLOSURE

Verified an independent partial formalization of the original conormal
route: actual determinant incidence and isolatedness, smooth Gauss-point
persistence, local flatness algebra, multiplicity-one algebra, and the
conditional cycle/Hilbert/numerical assembly. The exact results additionally
assume an unconstructed general Gauss-degree theory. The border results
additionally assume an unconstructed family certificate and fibrewise
representations. Construction of the actual conormal schemes and family,
their cycles and multidegrees, generic section realization, the classical
degree formula, and geometric conservation remain explicit obligations.
There is no new original-route theorem from bare border membership.
