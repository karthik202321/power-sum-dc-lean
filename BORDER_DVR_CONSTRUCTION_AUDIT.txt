BORDER DVR CONSTRUCTION AUDIT
=============================

Scope
-----
This audit concerns the construction of an algebraic determinant family from
literal border membership. It does not claim the final border degree bound.
The construction uses the original conormal-specialization route. It introduces
no custom axioms, sorry/admit proofs, or Kumar--Volk explicit-slice argument.

Input and coefficient-space control
-----------------------------------
The paper-facing inputs are the existing predicates:
  BorderRepresentation.OrdinaryFiniteSupportBorder n m
  BorderRepresentation.SymmetricFiniteSupportBorder n m
and their canonical OrdinaryBorderAtSize / SymmetricBorderAtSize versions.

Finite-support border membership means that an actual coefficient vector whose
polynomial is Fermat lies in the Euclidean closure of the actual ordinary or
symmetric determinant locus in some finite coefficient space.

BorderDvrFamily.coefficientReindex is the literal map obtained by reconstructing
a polynomial in the first coefficient space and reading its coefficients in the
canonical symbolic support. Its continuity is proved coefficient by coefficient.
BorderDvrFamily.actual_determinant_support_subset proves that this canonical
support contains every actual size-m determinant, using specialization of the
universal pencil. Support containment is not supplied as a new hypothesis.

Coefficient reindexing sends the original determinant locus into the canonical
locus and sends the distinguished coefficient vector to the canonical Fermat
vector. Continuity then transfers Euclidean closure membership. The already
proved inclusion of Euclidean closure in Zariski closure supplies canonical
Zariski border membership.

No reverse closure comparison is assumed or proved here. In particular none of
these endpoints takes ZariskiBridge.UniversalPolynomialImageClosure as input,
and this audit does not claim equality of Euclidean and Zariski closures.

Actual curve, field extension, and normalized local branch
---------------------------------------------------------
For an omitted point of the polynomial image, the construction applies actual
algebraic curve selection through that point. The selected curve is an integral
one-dimensional quotient, finite over a polynomial parameter ring. Its generic
point has an actual preimage under the original polynomial parameterization.

GenericPolynomialLift constructs one finite extension of the curve's fraction
field containing a simultaneous solution for all representation parameters.
Thus the generic matrix entries form one coherent representation; independent
pointwise choices of matrices are not substituted for a family.

FiniteCurveExtension takes the actual integral closure of the curve ring in
that finite generic representation field. Finiteness follows from the
characteristic-zero finite-extension normalization argument over the parameter
PID. A point above the specified boundary point is constructed. Localization
there is proved to be a discrete valuation ring with residue field C.

The generic embedding of that local ring into the representation field is
injective. Every element of the representation field is proved to be a fraction
of local-ring elements. DeterminantDvrFamily.Family.isFractionRing therefore
identifies the representation field as the fraction field of this normalized
DVR, with the algebra structure specified by the actual embedding. It is not
silently identified with the smaller pre-extension curve fraction field.

Literal determinant polynomial and specialization
--------------------------------------------------
The coefficient functions lie in the actual DVR. Their finite reconstruction is
an actual multivariate polynomial over that ring. Mapping it through the
residue homomorphism gives exactly PolynomialModel.fermat n. Mapping it through
the generic embedding gives exactly PolynomialModel.determinant of the generic
matrix pencil. These are polynomial equalities, not only equality of zero sets
or assumed coefficient identities.

The symmetric construction proves symmetry of the generic matrix pencil. Its
pole-cleared regular pencil is also symmetric. Representation entries may have
poles at the boundary point: the construction does not assert that the original
generic matrix entries themselves specialize. Instead an actual uniformizer
and one finite exponent are constructed whose power simultaneously clears all
matrix-entry poles. Coefficient regularity and exact Fermat specialization hold
before this pole clearing.

Principal paper-facing endpoints
-------------------------------
In namespace Sheshadri.BorderDvrFamily:
  ordinary_finiteSupportBorder_zariski
  symmetric_finiteSupportBorder_zariski
  ordinary_finiteSupportBorder_exact_or_family
  symmetric_finiteSupportBorder_exact_or_family
  ordinary_borderAtSize_exact_or_family
  symmetric_borderAtSize_exact_or_family

The exact-or-family conclusions separate an already-existing complex exact
representation from a genuine boundary case. In the boundary case they produce
the constructed normalized DVR family, with actual quotient and localization
rings, the generic determinant equality, residue Fermat equality, fraction-field
property, and uniformizer pole bound. These properties are derived from literal
border membership, not passed in as a family-specific certificate.

Validation and remaining obligations
------------------------------------
The modules CurveNormalization, GenericPolynomialLift, FiniteCurveExtension,
DvrRationalEntries, AlgebraicBorderFamily, DeterminantDvrFamily, and
BorderDvrFamily passed Lean builds. A separate audit of sixteen principal
construction and endpoint declarations reported only Lean's standard axioms:
  propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

This construction is not a proof of the final numerical border theorem.
The incidence/degree estimate over the actual generic representation field
still requires its applicable proof or a justified transfer to a suitable
complex fibre. The required projective cycle/intersection identification and
its connection to the constructed family likewise remain separate obligations.
Those conclusions must not be inferred merely from the existence of this DVR
family or from the clean axiom audit. No analytic accumulation theorem is
claimed by the algebraic normalization construction itself.
