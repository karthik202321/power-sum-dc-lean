END-TO-END HYPOTHESES AND STATEMENT AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-10

Purpose and limits
------------------
This is a semantic audit of the elaborated declarations and their mathematical
inputs, independent of the foundation-axiom audit. A clean axiom report alone
does not discharge theorem hypotheses. Compiler and foundation results are
recorded separately in the package build and axiom logs.

Compiler confirmation: an independent scratch Lean file imported the built
Sheshadri.ConormalBorderMultiplicity module and checked the fully explicit
types with both pp.explicit and pp.universes enabled. Lean exited with code 0.
The eight declarations checked were ordinary_border_quadratic,
symmetric_border_quadratic, ordinary_borderAtSize_quadratic,
symmetric_borderAtSize_quadratic, ordinary_border_count,
symmetric_border_count, ordinary_zariski_count, and symmetric_zariski_count.
Their only quantified parameters are the natural numbers n and m; their
only hypotheses are 3 <= n and the displayed literal border predicate.
There are no hidden implicit typeclass, family, or geometric parameters.

The audited end-to-end target is the ordinary and symmetric Fermat border
lower bound for n >= 3. The implementation specializes actual local Gauss
sections over a constructed DVR and compares their full scheme lengths.
It is not a claim that every global cycle identity, every general theorem,
or every line of the manuscript has been formalized verbatim.

Final endpoint statements
-------------------------
In Sheshadri.ConormalBorderMultiplicity, n and m are natural numbers.

ordinary_border_quadratic:
  3 <= n -> OrdinaryFiniteSupportBorder n m ->
  ((n : Real) - 1)^2 / (4 * Real.exp 1) <= (m : Real)

symmetric_border_quadratic:
  3 <= n -> SymmetricFiniteSupportBorder n m ->
  ((n : Real) - 1)^2 / (2 * Real.exp 1) <= (m : Real)

The corresponding ordinary_borderAtSize_quadratic and
symmetric_borderAtSize_quadratic endpoints use the canonical coefficient
space containing the target and every size-m determinant. Count endpoints
also prove fermatCount n <= ordinaryBezout m n or symmetricBezout m n.
The Zariski count endpoints assume literal Zariski-border membership.

These elaborated statements have no family, GaussDegreeTheory, finite-section,
cycle, transversality, Bezout, preservation, or closure-comparison argument.
The family-level helper does accept an actual determinant DVR family, but
the border endpoint constructs that family from its border hypothesis.

Meaning of border membership
----------------------------
BorderRepresentation.OrdinaryFiniteSupportBorder is an existential finite
coefficient space in which the actual Fermat coefficient vector lies in the
Euclidean closure of the locus of actual affine-linear size-m determinants.
Symmetric membership restricts the actual matrices to symmetric pencils.
The target polynomial is literally sum_i X_i^n.

The canonical AtSize predicates use coefficientSupport n m. The development
proves that this support contains every size-m determinant and the target,
and identifies the exact locus with the coefficient image of all pencils.
Thus these predicates do not impose an unproved support restriction.

BorderDvrFamily uses only the proved inclusion from Euclidean closure into
the common zero set of all vanishing polynomials. It does not assume equality
of Euclidean and Zariski closures, or that those closures agree for arbitrary
sets. The actual curve/normalization construction supplies the DVR, its
residue map, its fraction-field embedding, and the determinant family.

Special section and the lower degree
-----------------------------------
ordinary_family_count and symmetric_family_count both invoke
ConormalGaussSection.exists_section. They do not accept a Section record from
the caller. The hypotheses of that constructor are discharged for the
literal Fermat form by homogeneity, nonzero degree, and its nonvanishing
gradient away from the vertex.

The intermediate Section record contains actual flags, distinct actual
roots, finite actual quotient algebras, Artinian localized intersection
rings, smoothness at the selected points, and a length-sum identity. This
record is proved to exist. Its count identity is not a numerical premise of
the final border theorem.

Generic section finiteness is constructed
----------------------------------------
ActualGenericGaussSection.exists_actual_section is applied to the actual
family and the already constructed special Section. It constructs base-ring
flag coefficients with precisely the requested residues. Associated-prime
avoidance provides regular generic cuts on the successive saturated ideals.
The primal flag is normalized by a proved unit of the base ring whose
residue is -1; normalization preserves the prescribed special coefficients.

The homogeneous family polynomial is an actual homogenization of the DVR
polynomial. Dehomogenization and derivatives recover the actual generic
determinant and its derivatives. Its initial Hilbert degree is bounded by
n-1 using a proved hypersurface Hilbert calculation. The n-2 polar cuts and
one primal cut reduce the eventual Hilbert function to a constant. The
actual affine quotient is then proved finite-dimensional.

The constructed generic ideal J satisfies
  Igen <= J <= saturation(Igen, gradient(det A)),
and the construction also proves equality with that saturation. Igen is the
literal ideal of det A, the n-2 chosen polar equations, and the primal chart
equation. No finite-dimensionality hypothesis about the full unsaturated
generic polar quotient is needed. That full quotient may have excess
components.

The saturation is defined by power membership for each displayed open
condition. It is an ideal contraction from those opens, not radicalization.
The code proves equality with the original ideal after localizing where any
one displayed condition is a unit. Nilpotent structure is retained.

Smoothness is proved, including geometric support
------------------------------------------------
SaturatedChartSmoothness constructs a homogeneous regular combination of
the derivative system. It remains regular on the homogeneous affine chart
and becomes a unit because that chart algebra is finite-dimensional.
ActualGenericGaussSection transfers this to a unit derivative combination
in the actual quotient by J.

ArbitraryFieldFinitePolarBound extends scalars to an algebraic closure of
the actual generic field. Tensor/quotient equivalences prove finiteness and
preserve the quotient dimension. The unit derivative combination stays a
unit, so every support point over the algebraic closure has a nonzero
derivative. The proof uses the proved inclusion for mapped saturations;
it does not assume saturation commutes with base change.

Full singular special multiplicities persist
-------------------------------------------
DVRRootPersistence starts with the literal square special equation system
and an actual isolated Artinian local intersection ring. It derives the
special regular sequence and regularity of the actual uniformizer in the
total local equation quotient. It then cancels generic denominators.

DVRSaturatedPersistence extends this to the saturated generic ideal. At a
chosen smooth special point, one actual derivative is a unit in the full
special local ring. Power membership followed by unit cancellation shows
that every contracted generic equation annihilates this full local ring.
The applied Gauss theorem proves localized ideal equality, not merely
equality of zero sets or preservation of residue fields.

DVRSectionLength uses simultaneous interpolation onto the actual local
algebras to bound their length sum by the actual special quotient dimension.
The contracted generic ideal gives a torsion-free DVR quotient.
DVRFibreDimension then bounds its residue dimension by its finite generic
dimension. It does not infer finite generic fibre from finite special fibre,
and does not assert finite generation of the total module over the DVR.

Bezout has the appropriate hypotheses and is proved
--------------------------------------------------
FieldSingularBezoutWithExcess.isolated_multiplicity_bound assumes actual
multihomogeneous equations, the square ambient-dimension equality, valid
projective charts, actual zero equations, Artinian actual local intersection
rings, and projective distinctness of the selected points. Its conclusion
bounds the sum of their full local lengths by the multihomogeneous
coefficient. It allows excess components away from the selected isolated
points. It assumes no simplicity, flatness, global finiteness, genericity,
or geometric counting theorem.

The proof constructs a split comparison system, proves its multigraded
Hilbert bound, and proves local persistence in the interpolating family.
The generic coefficient field need only have characteristic zero; it is
not silently identified with the complex numbers.

FieldPolarMultiplicityBound constructs determinant kernel lifts, a common
ordinary reduction, and actual local incidence-ring equivalences at smooth
isolated polar roots. It applies the proved singular Bezout theorem.
FieldFinitePolarBound recovers all support points of the actual finite
quotient and sums their complete local lengths. At each support point,
the saturation sandwich identifies the local J algebra with the original
polar algebra. Hence the upper bound counts full multiplicities, rather
than counting reduced points while discarding nilpotents.

Legacy conditional interfaces
-----------------------------
Older modules retain definitions and theorems parameterized by
GaussDegreeTheory, FamilyCertificate, or UniversalPolynomialImageClosure.
Their presence as reusable conditional interfaces is not a proof that their
inputs have been discharged. Conversely, their import does not make them
assumptions of every declaration in an importing module.

The new ConormalBorderMultiplicity endpoint proof terms instead use the
constructed local-section route described above. They do not apply those
legacy geometric principles. Claims about the older global-cycle interfaces
must be distinguished from the now assembled bound endpoints.

Method and provenance
---------------------
The inspected endpoint dependency route chooses Gauss-polar flags by
associated-prime avoidance and specializes their local intersection
algebras. It does not use a fixed Fermat coordinate slice x_(n-1) = 1 to
obtain the target count. Setting the homogenizing coordinate x_0 = 1 is
the ordinary affine chart for the represented polynomial; the primal flag
coefficients themselves are constructed with prescribed residues.

This audit establishes which mathematical constructions occur in the code.
It does not establish historical provenance, originality, or independence
from another author's ideas. The accurate method description is a local
Gauss-section implementation of conormal/Gauss specialization, rather than
a verbatim formalization of the manuscript's global cycle presentation.
